# Campaign Doors

**Status: PLANNED.** The owner approved this experience direction for a later release. It is not part of V0.1.1; this document approves no artwork, final motion timing, editor catalog, or implementation.

## Campaign collection

Campaigns appear like folders in Windows, arranged in a simple responsive grid of large illustrated doors. Do not build a virtual three-dimensional corridor or camera navigation through a space. Each door has its own appearance. Under it, show the campaign title and a one- or two-line description. Keep the page atmospheric while preserving a useful amount of information on screen. The surrounding page remains an ordinary, usable themed website.

Masters will be able to customize their campaign door. Doors can represent any genre or setting, not only medieval fantasy. The materials, decoration catalog, editor controls, and final visual assets remain open.

## Interaction direction

- On hover, a door opens slightly and light begins to pass through the gap. The light color may differ by campaign. A subtle glow beneath the door is also allowed.
- Hovering a campaign title reveals a short story phrase. Clicking the title opens a modal with full adventure information.
- Clicking an available door plays the opening transition, darkens the screen, and opens that campaign's lobby. It does not enter the game table directly.
- A separate, plain, slightly crooked door creates a campaign. Its caption is **«Создать новое приключение»**.
- Dust in light beams and rare black-cat or bird animations are optional decorative details. They must not interfere with interaction. Provide a way to disable decoration and honor reduced motion. Do not run costly animations continuously. Exact durations and performance budget remain undecided.

## Campaign Lobby

The lobby is a standalone campaign page before a game session begins. Players can add a character; GM music and chat are available; other preparation and social actions may be added. It is not the live tabletop. The full composition and interaction set remain open design decisions.

## Adventure Keys and access

Keys are a future visual metaphor for server-side access rights. Private campaigns are not listed publicly; the GM invites named players, and an invitee then gains access to that campaign door. Public campaigns may appear in a catalog. A GM may grant free access; paid access is a possible later concept. A key never replaces authentication or authorization and must not become a transferable bearer credential without a separate security design.

## Public GM marketplace

The later concept includes public GMs and adventures, player/GM discovery, free or paid sessions, ratings, reviews of GMs and players, and granting or acquiring adventure access. Private friend groups must be able to use the platform without public listing or mandatory public ratings.

Payments, GM payouts, cancellations, refunds, fees, moderation, terms of service, and commercial rules need separate product, legal, operational, and security design. No financial operation is approved here.

## Delivery boundary

Campaign Doors, Door Studio, Campaign Lobby, Adventure Keys, catalog, and marketplace remain `PLANNED`. Do not implement UI, endpoints, schemas, payment placeholders, or technical identifier renames in V0.1.1. Update product vision and roadmap only to record these future directions. Existing V0.1.1 gates do not change.
